# E-mail as messages

A belfry receives mail for its members and channels, and members answer from the chat.

| Address | Goes to |
|---|---|
| `<username>@<domain>` | the member (active pass), in the chat's **Messages** |
| `<channel>@<domain>` | the channel, group or event chat, posted as the member who sent it (members only, and the mail must pass DKIM or SPF aligned with its From). The post carries `client belfry-mail`, a `subject` tag and `mail-original` (the whole e-mail, AES-GCM on Blossom: url, key, nonce); the chat shows it as the member's message with a small "via e-mail" on its time line ("Full e-mail" is in the message's menu). It also carries `message-id`, `in-reply-to` and `references`. When its In-Reply-To / References name the `message-id` of something in the channel (an e-mail card, what went out, another member's mail), it is posted as a reply in that message's thread instead (NIP-22 kind 1111, `E`/`K`/`P` + `e`/`k`/`p` = that message, or the message a matched reply hangs off) |
| `postmaster@`, `abuse@` | the admins' Messages |

Usernames win over channel names.

## Shared inboxes

A channel's admins can turn it into a **shared inbox** (channel settings in the chat; a NIP-78 kind 30078 event in the group, `d` = `belfry:inbox:<channel>` and `h` = the channel, content `{"on": true}`; only its admins' setting counts; the latest wins). Until 2026-11-21 the `d` was `belfry:inbox` alone: a 30078 is addressable by kind, author and `d`, so an admin's setting for one channel replaced their setting for every other (OSV's #hello lost its inbox when #sponsors got one). Readers still take an old `belfry:inbox` event for the channel in its `h`; writers write only the new `d`. Every refused mail is logged ("mail: refused for channel: 550 …", never the address), and pictures put inline in a mail are kept like attachments. Then:

- mail from **anyone** to `<channel>@<domain>` lands in the channel as an **e-mail**: a kind 9 posted by the groups key with `client belfry-mail`, `mail-from`, `mail-name`, `subject`, `message-id`, `in-reply-to`, `references` (since 2026-11-16: In-Reply-To and References, at most 10 ids, space-separated), `belfry-auth` and `mail-original`. The chat trusts it only when the groups key posted it; that key is published as `_groups@<domain>` in nostr.json. Mail from a member's **proven** address (DKIM or SPF aligned) still shows as their own message; a member's address without that proof is treated as anyone's (it could be forged), and the bridge logs which of the two it was;
- **e-mail threads** (2026-11-16, Xavier: don't pollute the channel): the chat joins a channel's e-mails into threads on each device, nothing more on the wire. An e-mail naming one already in a thread (In-Reply-To / References → its `message-id`) joins that thread; else the same sender with the same subject (Re:, Fwd: … aside) joins theirs; else it starts one and is its **root** (the id its route `#/c/<id>/thread/<root>`, labels and replies use). What went out joins the thread of the e-mail it answers (`e`). The channel's feed shows **one row per thread** where its latest activity is (sender, subject, a line of the latest e-mail, state, who has it, counts); a follow-up moves the row. Older cards need no migration: each is a root or joins one;
- the thread holds the e-mails in frames (received / sent) and the team's talk as ordinary **NIP-22 replies** (kind 1111 in the group, under the root; replies older clients hung under a later e-mail of the thread count too). Replies never leave the belfry;
- since 2026-11-22 the cards carry `to` / `cc` tags and replies go to everyone (Reply all); drafts and the automatic "answered" are in [one-shape.md](one-shape.md);
- **answering**: the thread has one composer with a line above it. By default "Message will be sent in this thread" (a NIP-22 reply). The channel's **admins** (inbox on) also see "Reply by e-mail instead": the line becomes "This will be sent to <name> by e-mail", with From = the channel's address and the subject, the composer turns bronze, and sending waits 8 seconds for Undo. The app sends a kind 1301 with `mail-from` = the channel's address and `e` = the latest e-mail received (In-Reply-To = its Message-ID, References = the thread's earlier ones) to the bridge, which checks that the sender is one of the group's admins and has an active pass, sends it signed "<name> for <channel>", and posts what went out in the channel (kind 9, `client belfry-mail-out`, `sent-by`, `mail-to`, `message-id`, `e`). Only the bridge posts these, so a note can never pass for something the sender received. Then the composer is back in thread mode;
- the channel's address: its **plain alias** (`hello@` for `hello-79f0`) when it has one (a channel a belfry admin made, the only one answering to that name: `mail.channel_by_slug`, the chat's `mailLocal`), else its id. Both forms receive; answers go out from the plain one (envelope and From header);
- a thread is Open, Answered (after an answer), Open again when the sender writes back, or Closed, and someone can take it: NIP-32 labels (kind 1985, namespace `belfry/inbox`: `l open|closed|assigned`, `e` = the thread's root (older labels on any of its e-mails count, latest wins), `p` = who has it). Anything else is refused at `RCPT TO` (550).

## Forwarding

For a transition (an old mailbox moves into a channel but must keep getting every mail; Xavier, 2026-11-17), a channel's admins can set **"Forward all e-mails to"** an outside address (channel settings in the chat, the e-mail part). Every mail the channel takes, at its plain alias or its id, is still posted, and a copy goes to that address (`belfry_onboarding/mailforward.py`).

- **The setting** is the belfry's, never a Nostr event (every member would read the address): register param `belfry.mail_forward.<channel id>` (JSON: address, state, set_by, since, a hash of the pending token). REGISTER=belfry only; with Odoo the API answers `409 unsupported`.
- **API** `POST belfry.<domain>/api/v1/mail-forward`, NIP-98, by someone who may edit the channel (the `/group-meta` rule: the group's admins, admins by role, the belfry's admins; a member's pass, never a guest or an agent). Bodies: `{"channel"}` → the state; `{"channel", "address": "x@y"}` → set, state `pending`, the confirmation mail goes out; `{"channel", "address": null}` → off; `{"channel", "resend": true}`. Answer `{"forward": {"address", "state": "pending"|"on", "set_by", "since", "same_domain_skipped"} | null}`. Errors: `403 not_allowed`, `404 unknown_channel`, `422 invalid`, `422 own_domain` (the belfry's domain or any of its hosts: loops), `409 archived` (setting or resending; turning off still works), `409 not_pending` (resend with nothing waiting), `429 too_many` (3 confirmation mails per channel per hour), `503 no_mail` (no provider, or the confirmation could not be sent). Setting the same address that is already `on` changes nothing; a different one starts over at `pending`.
- **Confirmation**: nothing is forwarded until the outside address confirms. The mail says that <belfry>'s channel <name> wants to forward its e-mails there, with `https://belfry.<domain>/mail-forward/<token>` (random, 7 days, single use). GET shows the names (from the stored record, never from the URL) and one button; only the POST confirms (mail scanners open links). The page says how to stop: the channel's admins turn it off. No unsubscribe link in v1.
- **The copy** is the original message with its header block edited in place; the body and attachments are passed through byte for byte (never re-encoded, never wrapped as message/rfc822), and Message-ID, In-Reply-To, References, Subject, Date, To and Cc stay exactly as they were. Changes: `From: "<original name, else its address> via <alias>@<domain>" <alias@domain>` (always: the provider signs only for our domain, and the original's DMARC would fail anyway); `Reply-To:` and `X-Original-From:` = the original From; the original Reply-To, if any, moves to `X-Original-Reply-To`; the original's `DKIM-Signature`, `ARC-*`, `Return-Path` and `Sender` go; `X-Forwarded-For-Channel: <alias>@<domain>` and `X-Belfry-Forward: <domain>` are added. `Auto-Submitted` is left alone.
- **Envelope**: rcpt = the forward address, mail from = `bounces@<domain>`, through the same SMTP provider as the outbound bridge (plain SMTP, so the provider keeps our Message-ID). Mail to `bounces@` (bounces, auto-replies) is taken, logged and dropped: never posted, never forwarded. `bounces` is a reserved username.
- **When**: after the mail is posted, by any route (a member's message, a member's thread reply, a shared-inbox card), once per mail even when it named both the alias and the id. Best effort in a background thread: 3 tries (now, +1 min, +4 min) on 4xx or a network error, none after a 5xx (a size refusal is logged as "too big"; no splitting). A failed forward never touches the post. Logs say the channel and the address's domain only.
- **Loop guards** — not forwarded: a mail carrying `X-Belfry-Forward: <this domain>`; a mail from the forward address itself; a mail from this belfry's domain or one of its hosts (e.g. the channel's own address); a mail from the forward address's domain (the outside mailbox answering as hello@ through its own provider) **unless** that domain is a public mailbox provider (gmail.com, googlemail.com, outlook.com, hotmail.com, live.com, msn.com, yahoo.com, ymail.com, icloud.com, me.com, mac.com, proton.me, protonmail.com, protonmail.ch, pm.me, gmx.com, gmx.net, gmx.de, aol.com, fastmail.com, fastmail.fm, mail.ru, yandex.com, yandex.ru, zoho.com), where it would drop everyone's mail; the chat says so under the setting (`same_domain_skipped`). At most 200 forwards per channel per hour (logged when it hits).
- **Not forwarded**: mail refused at SMTP time (not posted), mail while the forward is `pending`, mail to members' own addresses, postmaster@ / abuse@, and what the channel sends out.
- The native apps (iOS, Android) don't have this setting yet; the web chat does.

## Protocol

There is no numbered NIP for e-mail. Belfry follows **Nostr Mail** ([nogringo/nostr-mail](https://github.com/nogringo/nostr-mail)), the convention Nostr mail clients use:

- an e-mail is a kind **1301** rumor whose content is the RFC 2822 message, gift-wrapped with NIP-59 like a NIP-17 DM, and published on the recipient's DM relays (kind 10050: the community relay);
- the belfry is the bridge, `_smtp@<domain>` in its nostr.json (the belfry key);
- incoming mail: a 1301 from the bridge with `mail-from` (the legacy sender), plus Belfry's `belfry-auth` tag (`pass` when DKIM or SPF aligned with From). A message whose rumor would pass 40 000 bytes of JSON (what fits a gift wrap, see [sealed.md](sealed.md) §8), and any message over 60 kB, is stored on Blossom encrypted with AES-GCM; the rumor carries `x`, `url`, `decryption-key` and `decryption-nonce` (Nostr Mail's large-MIME scheme);
- outgoing mail: the member's app wraps a 1301 with `email-id`, `mail-from` (their `username@domain`) and `rcpt-to` to the bridge, and a copy to themselves. The belfry checks that the address is the sender's and that the From header matches, removes Bcc, sends through the belfry's mail provider (`SMTP_*`, the one its system mails use) and answers with a kind **7679** delivery report (no `p` tag, encrypted to the sender with a throwaway key). The reports are also how a restart knows what was already sent.

Only what the sender wrote is kept in the message: the quoted history (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird; English, French, German, Spanish, Dutch, Italian, Portuguese headers), the signature after `-- ` and phone footers ("Sent from my iPhone") are cut. The whole e-mail stays one tap away.

The chat app reads NIP-17 (kind 14), old NIP-04 (kind 4) and Nostr Mail in one Messages list.

## Sealed mail

Sealed conversations ([sealed.md](sealed.md), §7) change three things here, and nothing for a member without a ready chain of devices:

- **In.** Mail for `<username>@` whose member has a ready chain is sealed to their head letterbox: the same kind 1301 rumor, the seal still signed by the belfry key, the gift wrap encrypted to the letterbox key and tagged `["lb", <letterbox>]` next to `["p", <member>]`. Only the member's devices open it; the signer cannot. If the relay does not answer when the belfry reads the member's chain, the mail is refused for now (451) and the sending server tries again: it is never delivered unsealed because of a failure.
- **Out.** The wrap to the belfry key is unchanged, but its seal may be signed by one of the member's devices instead of the account key (the rumor's `pubkey` stays the member). The belfry takes it when the device is in the head of the member's valid chain and is the `client_pubkey` of one of their live chat sessions in the signer (`"chat": true` in `GET /api/me/sessions`). Anything else is dropped without a report, like any wrap whose seal is not its author's. This holds for a shared inbox's answers too. Seals by the account key are taken as before. When the relay or the signer does not answer during that check, the wrap is tried again after 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, then every 30 min for a day (in memory; after a restart the last three days are read again).
- **Report.** The kind 7679 report is encrypted to the sender's head letterbox, with an `["lb", <letterbox>]` tag, when their chain is ready; else to their account key as before. The belfry reads the chain before the mail leaves: when that read fails nothing is sent yet and the wrap is tried again as above, so a report never goes to the account key because of a failure.

The code is `belfry_onboarding/sealed.py`; the vectors all clients share are `docs/sealed-vectors.json` (written by `services/onboarding/tools/sealed_vectors.py`).

## Running it

- `mail-in` (Compose, `belfry_onboarding/mailfront.py`) listens on port 25. It runs as Caddy's user and uses Caddy's certificate for STARTTLS. It keeps nothing: every address and message goes to the onboarding service (`/internal/mail/*`, hook token), and a sender whose mail could not be delivered tries again later (4xx).
- DNS: `MX <domain> 10 <domain>.` (the apex already points at the box). Sending needs nothing more than what the mail provider already asked for (SPF/DKIM for the sender domain).
- The firewall: install.sh opens 25/tcp. Docker publishes it anyway.
- `MAIL_IN=0` in `infra/.env` turns receiving and the bridge off.
- Tests: `tests/test_mail_e2e.py` sends a Gmail-shaped mail (text, HTML with a pasted picture, a PDF) over SMTP to the real mail-in, which hands it over real HTTP to the onboarding app; the card, its three Blossom uploads (picture, PDF, the whole e-mail encrypted) and the forward through a local SMTP provider are checked, and an IPv6 sender too.
- Limits: 15 MB per message (`MAIL_MAX_BYTES`), 20 recipients, 20 messages per sender address per 10 minutes, 60 sent mails per member per hour.

## Turning a shared inbox on or off from the server

`belfry inbox <channel>` prints whether the channel takes mail from anyone, as the server reads it now (`mail.inbox_now`: `inbox_on` without its one-minute cache). `belfry inbox <channel> on|off` publishes the setting itself, no client needed: a kind 30078 with `d` = `belfry:inbox:<channel>`, `h` = the channel and `{"on": true|false}`, signed by the **groups key** (it is in every channel's 39001, so `inbox_on` counts it like an admin's), dated after any setting already there (a replaceable event of the same second: the lower id wins), then reads the state back and prints it. It exits 1 when the read-back differs (the groups key is not among that channel's admins) and 2 for an unknown channel. Underneath: `python -m belfry_onboarding inbox <channel> [on|off]` in the onboarding container. The console has no channel or mail page yet, so there is no button for it there.

## Health check

Incoming mail broke silently twice (2026-11-21 a shared-inbox setting replaced by another channel's; 2026-11-22 mail-in listening on IPv4 only, every IPv6 sender refused, Gmail first). So the belfry checks itself (`belfry_onboarding/mailcheck.py`, `belfry_onboarding/smtpprobe.py`):

- **What.** A probe talks to port 25 of the MX host, once per address family (A, AAAA), the way a sending server does: `EHLO mailcheck.<domain>`, `MAIL FROM:<mailcheck@<domain>>`, `RCPT TO:<postmaster@<domain>>`, `RSET`, `QUIT`. Never `DATA`: nothing is delivered or posted. 20 s timeout per step. The `RCPT` goes through mail-in to `/internal/mail/rcpt`, so a backend that does not answer (451) fails too. A `5xx` for postmaster@ passes only while the belfry has no admin (postmaster@ goes to the admins).
- **Where it runs: on the host.** A container on Docker's edge bridge cannot reach the box's own public IPv6 on port 25 (Docker's DNAT rule skips traffic that starts on that bridge: belfry.sh, 2026-11-22, a timeout from inside, 250 from the host), so the probe is `belfry_onboarding/smtpprobe.py`, standard library only, run on the host as a plain script by **`belfry-mailcheck.timer`** every 5 minutes (installed by `belfry setup-units`; `belfry mailcheck-timer`). It connects to what the onboarding service wrote in `infra/data/setup/mailcheck-target.json` (the service has DNS: `{at, domain, active, reason?, host, addresses: {v4, v6}, no_admin}`), or, when there is none or it is older than a day, the domain's own addresses. It writes `infra/data/setup/mailcheck.json`: `{active, at, host, v4: {ok, detail, at, ip, ms?}, v6: {…}}` (mode 664; the folder is mounted in the onboarding container at `/belfry-setup`). When the target says the check is not wanted it probes nothing and writes nothing.
- **Rounds.** Every 5 minutes (from the followers tick, in its own thread; not in the first minute after a start) the onboarding service resolves the MX, rewrites the target, and takes each family from `mailcheck.json` when that is at most **15 minutes** old. A family without a fresh host result is probed from the onboarding container itself, where a family it has no route for (IPv6 on the default network) is "not checked", never a failure.
- **When it runs.** `MAIL_IN` is on, `MAILCHECK` is not `0`, the domain is a public one (not `.test`, `.local`, `localhost`, an IP…), and the MX points at this belfry: the MX host is the domain itself, or shares an address with it (the apex points at the box). An MX that points elsewhere (mail still at another provider) is never connected to. Without an MX, only with `MAILCHECK=1` (then the domain itself, RFC 5321's implicit MX) or once a check has passed here before. `MAILCHECK` is an `infra/.env` setting passed to the onboarding container.
- **The result** per family, in the register param `belfry.mailcheck` (JSON; in memory with Odoo): `ok` (true, false, or null for "not checked"), `detail` (the step and the server's answer, addresses removed), `since` (when that state began), `checked`, `fails` (failed rounds in a row), `told`.
- **Telling.** After **two** failed rounds in a row (one blip tells nobody), the belfry's admins get one e-mail ("Mail to <domain> is not arriving over IPv6", with each failed step) and one push of type `mail_down` (fixed text, opens belfry.<domain>/status). Both families failing in the same round make one message. The first round that passes after that sends one more of each (`mail_up`, "Mail to <domain> arrives again over …"). Nothing in between. Every failed round is logged ("mailcheck: IPv6 to port 25 of <host> failed (2 in a row): connection refused"); no person's address is ever logged. An admin whose own address is on this belfry's domain may not get the e-mail while mail is down: the push and the status page still say it.
- **Not checked** (never a failure, never told): a family DNS gives no address for (no AAAA: no IPv6 row), and a family the prober has no route for (the connect fails at once with "network unreachable"). Without the timer (`belfry setup-units` not run since this landed), IPv6 is therefore "not checked" on most boxes.
- **Status page.** belfry.<domain>/status (and `status.json`) shows "Mail in (IPv4)" and "Mail in (IPv6)" rows with the last result, "(from the host)" when the host's probe gave it, and since when.
- **Deploys.** `belfry mailcheck` asks the onboarding service for a fresh target (`python -m belfry_onboarding mailcheck --target`), then runs the host probe and keeps its result; one line per family (`v4 ok: answers (12 ms) (<host>, IPv4, from the host)`, `v6 FAIL: connection refused (…)`, `v6 not checked: …`), exit 1 when a family fails. It tells nobody (the next round does). `belfry apply` runs it at the end (up to three tries, mail-in may still be starting) and prints a loud warning block when it fails; the apply itself never fails because of it. `python -m belfry_onboarding mailcheck` alone probes from inside the container (a debugging view: IPv6 there is not the host's).

DKIM, for the record: every `DKIM-Signature` of an incoming mail gets one INFO line, `mail: dkim d=<d> s=<s>: ok | ok, not aligned with From | fail (<why>) | <exception>`, and nothing else of the mail. A Google Workspace domain that never set up its own DKIM key is signed by Google as `d=<domain>.<date>.gappssmtp.com`: valid, but not aligned with From, so it does not count (SPF aligned still does). Tests sign real messages and send them through mail-in's SMTP path (`tests/test_mail_e2e.py`): the bytes onboarding verifies are the bytes that were signed.

## Not yet

- No content spam filter. Mail from unknown senders reaches members marked "unverified sender" when it has no aligned DKIM or SPF. Channels already require a member's proven address.
- SPF is skipped for mail arriving over IPv6. Docker's proxy hides the sender's address there, so DKIM is what counts.
- Members can't attach files to outgoing mail yet, and the bridge doesn't send large (Blossom) outgoing mail.
- A NIP-17 DM between members is not copied to e-mail.
- Shared inboxes: no "Ask for review" drafts yet, no rules or auto-assignment, no notification e-mails to the team. The native apps still show each e-mail as a card of its own (they don't join threads yet).
